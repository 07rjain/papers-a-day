# How do you turn a text predictor into an assistant?
A language model can finish a sentence. How do you teach it to answer a question,
judge when to refuse, and remember an instruction several turns later?
Llama Two tackles these problems by changing what the model practices,
and by changing how its answers are judged.

# Setup · Build a base model
Start on the left. Meta pretrained Llama Two on two trillion tokens.
A token is a piece of text. The model learned by predicting the next piece.
Its context window was four thousand ninety-six tokens.
The thirty-four and seventy billion parameter models used grouped-query attention.
Several query heads share keys and values, so generation can use a smaller cache.

# 1 · Demonstrate good answers
Move one step to the right. Human-written examples show the model
how a good assistant might respond.
The team collected twenty-seven thousand five hundred forty annotations
for supervised fine-tuning.
That gives the model a starting point. It can now draft replies
for people to compare, and those comparisons become the next training signal.

# 2 · Learn two kinds of feedback
One scorer learns which answers people find more helpful.
Another learns which answers people find safer.
The paper gives a routing rule for reward: use safety on a safety prompt,
or when the answer's safety score falls below zero point one five.
Otherwise use helpfulness. On borderline prompts, stronger safety tuning
could also make the assistant refuse harmless requests.

# 3 · Improve, then repeat
In rejection sampling, generate several replies and train on the best-scored ones.
In P P O, use the reward to update the probabilities of generated replies,
with a penalty for drifting too far from a reference model.
Better models produce new replies for people to compare.
The team iterated from R L H F version one to version five.

# 4 · Carry an instruction across turns
Now follow the same assistant into a conversation. Say the instruction is, answer in French.
Ghost Attention repeats the instruction while generating practice conversations.
For fine-tuning, it keeps the instruction only on the first turn
and trains on the final reply. Earlier turns provide context.
The paper reports consistency for twenty or more turns, up to the context limit.

# Lesson · Train the behavior you want to see
Meta released base and chat weights in seven, thirteen, and seventy billion
parameter sizes under the Llama Two license.
The lesson is simple. Show the model what good behavior looks like,
score the behaviors you care about, and practice them in the setting where they matter.
